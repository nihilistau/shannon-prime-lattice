# Judge K-sweep RESOLVED: K=1 breaks the reject, K=2 works (83% clean-reject); deploy = L5-direct deliver + judge@K2 reject-only

Empirical K-sweep of the pass→block judge on the served 12B (judge_test.py, N=12 in-mem paraphrase + 12 clean-v2 foreign; authority fix 4ff75d1 active; SP_B3_JUDGE=1 SP_B3_JUDGE_L5=1).

## Results
| config | PARA recall (obey) | FOREIGN spurious | clean-reject | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **K=1** | 6/12 = 50% | — (NULLs=0) | reject BROKEN | 1 candidate → judge degenerates to always-PICK, never NULLs |
| **K=2** | 6/12 = 50% | 2/12 = 17% | **83%** | reject WORKS, tighter/cheaper than K=8 |
| K=8 (prior) | ~100% picks (buggy delivery) | 2/12 = 17% | 83% | baseline (_passblock_resolved.md) |
| L5-direct (SP_RECALL_L5) | **86.89%** | — | no reject | best recall, no reject |

## The two findings
1. **The judge needs ≥2 candidates to reject.** At K=1 the L5 shortlist is a single candidate; the judge sees `[cand, NULL]` and just PICKs — NULLs=0, no reject. At K=2 it has a real comparison and engages its NULL/reject behavior → 83% clean-reject (== K=8, less compute). So **K=2 is the minimal working reject config**, not K=1. (Operator was right: "K=2 works.") My earlier K=1 "residual-pollution" root cause was WRONG — the reject break is candidate-count degeneracy, not cache state.
2. **Delivery through the judge path costs recall (~50% vs 86.89%), independent of K.** Direct proof: japan → L5-direct obeys ("won"), judge-path misses ("yen"), judge NOT NULLing it. Routing the delivery through the judge session is strictly weaker than L5-direct delivery.

## Deployable shape (ADR-002 §8.1 — confirmed by data)
**L5-direct OWNS delivery (86.89% recall) + judge@K=2 as a reject-ONLY veto (83% clean-reject).** Don't deliver through the judge. This gets full recall AND the reject; neither costs the other. The remaining build is the reorder so the judge returns only PASS/NULL and L5-direct executes the delivery in every PASS case (currently the judge-PICK branch still delivers).
